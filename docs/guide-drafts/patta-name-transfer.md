A patta is the Revenue Department's record of who holds a piece of land. Banks ask for it before a loan, the electricity board asks for it before a new connection, and buyers ask for it before they pay. Until it is in your name, every one of those steps is harder.

## When the patta has to change

The patta must be updated whenever the land changes hands:

- a **sale**
- a **gift** or **settlement** deed
- a registered **partition** among family members
- **inheritance**, when the owner dies

## Case 1: whole survey number, no sub-division

If the deed covers a whole survey number or an existing sub-division, the transfer is **automatic**. After the Sub-Registrar registers the sale deed, the details go to the Revenue Department, and the patta moves to the buyer's name without a separate application.

Check it yourself a few days after registration. On the Tamil Nadu e-Services portal (eservices.tn.gov.in), enter the district, taluk, village, survey number and sub-division, and download the patta. If it still shows the seller's name, apply as in Case 2.

## Case 2: part of a survey number, or inheritance

If you bought only part of a survey number, the land has to be **sub-divided** before a separate patta can be issued. Inherited land also needs an application. In both cases:

1. Apply on the **Tamil Nilam citizen portal** (tamilnilam.tn.gov.in/citizen) or at an **e-Sevai centre**, and pay the processing fee. [CHECK: current fee.]
2. Note the application number and track it on the same portal.
3. For sub-division, a surveyor measures the land and prepares the sub-division sketch.
4. The **Tahsildar** passes the order, and the new patta can then be downloaded.

## Documents to keep ready

- The registered sale, gift, settlement or partition deed
- The Encumbrance Certificate for the property
- The seller's or previous owner's patta
- Your identity and address proof
- For inheritance: the **death certificate** and the **legal heir certificate**

## If the transfer is refused or delayed

- **Check the reason** given on the portal. Most refusals are for mismatched survey numbers, names or extents between the deed and the revenue records.
- **Rejected:** an order of the Tahsildar can be appealed to the Revenue Divisional Officer. [CHECK: appeal forum and time limit.]
- **Disputed ownership:** if another person claims the land, the revenue officers will not decide who owns it. That has to be settled in a civil court, and the patta follows the court's decree.

## A patta is not a title deed

A patta shows who the revenue records treat as the holder. It does not, on its own, prove ownership. Before buying land, check the full chain of title documents and the Encumbrance Certificate, not only the patta. Our guide on checking land title before you buy covers that.

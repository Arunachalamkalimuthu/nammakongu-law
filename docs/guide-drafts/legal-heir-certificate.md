When a family member dies, almost every office the family deals with afterwards will ask for a legal heir certificate. It is usually the first document to get, and it is easier to apply early than to hold up every other step later.

## What it is used for

- Changing the **patta** and other revenue records into the heirs' names
- Transferring the **electricity**, water or property tax account
- Claiming **family pension**, provident fund or gratuity of the deceased
- Claiming **insurance** and smaller bank balances
- Continuing or defending a court case in place of the deceased

## Who issues it and how to apply

The **Tahsildar** of the taluk where the deceased ordinarily lived issues the certificate.

1. Apply on the **e-Sevai portal** (tnesevai.tn.gov.in) or at an **e-Sevai centre**. [CHECK: current service name and fee.]
2. List **every** legal heir, with their age and relationship. Leaving out an heir is the most common reason a certificate is later cancelled.
3. The **Village Administrative Officer** and the **Revenue Inspector** enquire with neighbours and verify the family.
4. The Tahsildar issues the certificate, which can be downloaded from the portal. [CHECK: the time limit in the citizen charter.]

## Documents to keep ready

- The **death certificate**. If the death was never registered, that has to be done first.
- Identity and address proof of the applicant
- Names, ages and relationships of all legal heirs
- A self-declaration or affidavit about the family [CHECK: affidavit format used locally]
- The ration card or another address proof of the deceased

## Who counts as a legal heir

That depends on the personal law of the deceased. For Hindus, Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists, the **Hindu Succession Act, 1956** names the **Class I heirs**:

- the widow
- the sons and daughters, married or unmarried, with **equal shares**
- the mother
- the children and widow of a son or daughter who died earlier

Only if there is no Class I heir do the Class II heirs, such as the father and brothers and sisters, inherit. Different rules apply to Muslims, Christians and Parsis.

## Legal heir certificate and succession certificate

| | Legal heir certificate | Succession certificate |
|---|---|---|
| Issued by | Tahsildar | Civil court |
| Law | Revenue Department procedure | Indian Succession Act, 1925 |
| Main use | Records, pension, utilities, patta | Collecting debts and securities: deposits, shares, loans owed to the deceased |
| Decides shares? | No | No, but the court decides who may collect |

Banks often accept a legal heir certificate for smaller balances, and ask for a succession certificate for larger amounts. [CHECK: worth giving a rough idea?]

## When the matter goes to court

- **Disputed heirs:** another person claims to be an heir, or the family disputes a second marriage or an adoption.
- **Unverified family:** the Tahsildar cannot verify the family, for example because the deceased lived elsewhere for many years.
- **Debts and securities:** a **succession certificate** is needed.
- **Dividing property:** a legal heir certificate names the heirs but does not divide anything. That needs a **registered partition deed** or a **partition suit**.

## A legal heir certificate does not decide who owns property

The certificate only records who the heirs are. It does not decide how property is shared, and it does not override a valid will. If the deceased left a will, the property passes under the will, and the executor or beneficiaries may need **probate** or **letters of administration** from the court.

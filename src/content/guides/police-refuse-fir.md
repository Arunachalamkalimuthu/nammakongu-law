Since 1 July 2024, criminal procedure in India is governed by the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)**, which replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure. The steps below use the new section numbers. Offences committed before that date are still charged under the Indian Penal Code.

## Your right to an FIR

When information discloses a **cognizable offence** (one where police can arrest without a warrant, such as theft, cheating or assault), the officer in charge must record it as an FIR under Section 173(1). In **Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh (2013)**, the Supreme Court held that registration in such cases is mandatory.

The BNSS adds two protections:

- **Zero FIR.** You can give the information at any police station, whatever the place of the offence. The station registers it and transfers it.
- **e-FIR.** Information can be given electronically. It is taken on record once you sign it within three days.

You are entitled to a **free copy of the FIR** immediately (Section 173(2)).

### Preliminary enquiry

For offences punishable with three years or more but less than seven years, Section 173(3) allows the police, with the permission of an officer of at least Deputy Superintendent rank, to make a preliminary enquiry for up to 14 days before deciding whether there is a prima facie case.

## Step 1: Write to the Superintendent of Police

If the station refuses, send the substance of your complaint **in writing** to the Superintendent of Police (or the Commissioner in a city like Coimbatore or Tiruppur) under **Section 173(4)**. Send it by registered post or deliver it and keep the acknowledgement. The SP can investigate personally or direct a subordinate officer to.

## Step 2: Apply to the Magistrate

If nothing happens, apply to the Judicial Magistrate under **Section 175(3)**. The application must:

- be supported by an **affidavit** from you, and
- show that you approached the Superintendent of Police under Section 173(4) first.

The Magistrate can make an enquiry and, after considering the police officer's submissions, order the police to register and investigate.

## Alternative: a private complaint

You can also file a **private complaint** directly before the Magistrate under **Section 223**. The Magistrate examines you and your witnesses on oath and decides whether to take cognizance. This route suits cases where you already have the evidence and do not need a police investigation.

## Non-cognizable offences

For non-cognizable offences, such as many forms of simple hurt or defamation, the police record the information and refer you to the Magistrate under Section 174. They cannot investigate without the Magistrate's order.

## What to keep

- A written, signed copy of your complaint with the date
- The acknowledgement from the police station or SP's office
- Any CSR (Community Service Register) receipt the station gives you
- Medical records, photographs, messages and witness details

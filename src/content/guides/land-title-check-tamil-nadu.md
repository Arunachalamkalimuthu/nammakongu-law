Most land disputes that reach the civil courts start with a document nobody read before the sale. A careful title check costs little next to a partition suit or a claim by an heir who never signed.

## 1. Encumbrance Certificate

Get the EC for the survey number from **TNREGINET**, the Tamil Nadu Registration Department portal, for at least 30 years. It shows registered sales, mortgages, gifts, settlements, leases and court attachments.

Check that:

- the last transaction on the EC is the seller's own purchase or inheritance
- there is no registered mortgage that has not been discharged
- the extent and survey numbers match the documents you were shown

An EC does not show everything. An equitable mortgage made by depositing title deeds with a bank may not appear, so ask to see the **original** documents, not copies.

## 2. Patta, chitta and the FMB sketch

On the Revenue Department's **e-services portal**, check:

- **Patta:** the seller's name should appear as owner for the survey and subdivision number.
- **Chitta and A-register:** the land's classification. Agricultural land, natham and government poramboke are treated very differently. Do not buy land classified as poramboke.
- **FMB sketch:** the field map showing the shape and boundaries of the survey number. Visit the site and compare the boundaries on the ground with the FMB and the sale deed.

If the patta is still in a previous owner's or a deceased parent's name, the patta transfer needs to be completed or made a condition of the sale.

## 3. Parent documents

Read every document in the chain of title back at least 30 years: sale deeds, settlement deeds, partition deeds, wills, gift deeds. Look for:

- **Inherited property.** Every legal heir must join the sale. Ask for the legal heir certificate and death certificates. A sale signed by only one brother can be challenged by the others.
- **Power of attorney sales.** The power of attorney should be registered, should authorise a sale, and the person who gave it should be alive on the sale date.
- **Minors' shares.** A minor's share needs the permission of the court before it can be sold.

## 4. Layout approval

For house plots, confirm the layout is approved by the **DTCP** or the local planning authority, and note the approval number. Plots in unapproved layouts have to be regularised before they can be registered or built on with a permit.

For flats and larger projects, check that the project is registered with **TNRERA**, the Tamil Nadu Real Estate Regulatory Authority.

## 5. Pending litigation and dues

- Search the **eCourts** portal by party name and survey number for pending suits.
- Ask for the latest property tax and electricity receipts in the seller's name.
- If the land is near a highway, railway or planned project, check for acquisition notifications.

## 6. Before registration

- Confirm the **guideline value** of the land on TNREGINET. Stamp duty and registration fees are calculated on the higher of the guideline value and the sale price.
- Pay the seller through traceable bank transfers and record each payment in the sale deed.
- After registration, apply for **patta transfer** to your name without delay.

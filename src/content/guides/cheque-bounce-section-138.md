A bounced cheque is one of the most common disputes in the textile and trading towns of the Kongu belt. The law gives the payee a strong remedy, but only if each step is taken on time. Missing a deadline by a day can end the case before it starts.

## When Section 138 applies

A complaint under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 needs all of these:

- The cheque was given to discharge a **legally enforceable debt or liability**. A cheque given as a gift or donation is not covered.
- It was presented to the bank **within its validity period**, which is three months from the date on the cheque.
- The bank returned it unpaid because the funds were insufficient or the amount exceeded the arrangement with the bank. Courts have also applied Section 138 to returns marked "account closed" and "payment stopped by drawer".

## The timeline

| Step | Deadline | Who acts |
|---|---|---|
| Present the cheque | Within 3 months of the cheque date | Payee |
| Send written demand notice | Within 30 days of receiving the return memo | Payee |
| Pay the cheque amount | Within 15 days of receiving the notice | Drawer |
| File the complaint | Within 1 month after the 15 days end | Payee |

Keep the original cheque, the bank's return memo, a copy of the notice, and proof of dispatch and delivery (postal receipt and tracking, or acknowledgement card). The case is built on these papers.

## The demand notice

The notice must demand payment of the **cheque amount**. It should state the cheque number, date, amount, bank, the date of return and the reason given in the memo. Send it by registered post with acknowledgement due, and by any other mode you can prove, such as speed post or courier with tracking. If the drawer refuses or avoids the letter, courts generally treat a notice sent to the correct address as served.

## Where to file

Section 142(2) fixes the court by **where the payee banks**. If you deposited the cheque in your account for collection, file in the court with jurisdiction over your bank branch. That means a Tiruppur trader who deposits a cheque in a Tiruppur branch files in Tiruppur, even if the drawer lives in another state.

## What the court can order

- **Punishment:** imprisonment up to two years, a fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both.
- **Interim compensation (Section 143A):** up to 20 percent of the cheque amount, payable to the complainant while the trial is pending.
- **Deposit on appeal (Section 148):** if the accused appeals a conviction, the appellate court can order a deposit of at least 20 percent of the fine or compensation.

The offence is **compoundable** under Section 147. Most cases end in a settlement, and the court records the compromise and closes the case.

## If you received a notice

If you are the drawer, do not ignore the notice. You have 15 days to pay, and paying within that window stops a valid complaint. If you dispute the debt, a written reply within the notice period records your defence early.

## Documents to bring to an advocate

- The original bounced cheque and the bank return memo
- Proof of the debt: invoices, ledger extracts, agreements, bank transfers, messages
- Your bank statement showing the presentation
- Any notice already sent, with postal receipts and tracking
